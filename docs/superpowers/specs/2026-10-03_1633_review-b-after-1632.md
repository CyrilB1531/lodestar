## The problem

The Review B after #1632, on `main` at `72d1366c`, measured every load and save against the
released 0.7.0 across 284 bytes to 8 MiB and reads of 1 to 81,920 bytes. Loads matched 0.7.0, but:

- an asynchronous save just past a mebibyte allocated about 1 MB more than 0.7.0 — 4,253,536 bytes
  against 3,236,504 at 1,080,263 — and made two writes, `SpillBuffer` clamping its first chunk at a
  mebibyte and opening a second of a mebibyte for the 32 KB left (#1633);
- past 1.1 MB, loads were back at 0.7.0's cost, giving up what 84d272cc had saved — 25.2 MB over a
  seekable load at 8 MiB against 9.5 MB — unrecorded (#1634);
- `EmbeddingIndex.SaveAsync` composed its ids head in a `SpillBuffer`, allocating up to 41% more
  than 0.7.0, which wrote it through a writer on the stream (#1635);
- reads of one byte were 12–14% slower than 0.7.0's `MemoryStream` loop (#1636);
- a dead doubling in `SegmentChain`, a test comment and the #1629 spec misstated what was built and
  measured (#1637), and the allocation test covered neither the asynchronous loads nor smaller
  reads (#1638).

## Decisions

- **A load grows as `MemoryStream` did until it holds a mebibyte, then takes mebibyte segments.**
  The first segment grows to the larger of what the read brings, twice its length and 256 bytes,
  up to one array, while it holds less than a mebibyte; past that, segments of a mebibyte. Below a
  mebibyte, and through the doubling that crosses it, that is 0.7.0's single array, handed over
  uncopied; past it, the artifact is a segment sequence and nothing is doubled again. This is the
  hybrid #1634 asked to be measured before adoption: against 0.7.0, across sizes and reads, sync and
  async, 273 cells within 16 KB, 75 below, none above.
- **A base64 token split across segments decodes segment by segment.** Measured first without it,
  the hybrid cost more than 0.7.0 where a large token — the idf block, the vector block — crossed a
  segment boundary: the reader copied the whole token to decode it, 4,875,392 bytes against
  3,145,824 for a 2 MiB index read asynchronously. `Base64Numbers` now decodes each segment straight
  into the destination, carrying a quantum a boundary cuts; anything not canonical falls back to the
  reader's own decode, so every refusal is unchanged.
- **`SpillBuffer`'s last chunk grows as `MemoryStream`'s buffer did while it holds less than a
  mebibyte; a new one starts at what the next write needs.** The writer flushes a mebibyte at a
  time, so 1.08 MB reaches it as a mebibyte and 32 KB: two chunks of exactly that, 3,236,648 bytes
  against 0.7.0's 3,236,504, in two writes where 0.7.0 made one — a contiguous single write would
  need a copy of the artifact, which is what cost the megabyte. Growing the first chunk past a
  mebibyte instead was measured as no better there, the first flush already filling it.
- **`EmbeddingIndex.SaveAsync` writes its head through a writer on the stream, as 0.7.0 did,
  awaiting a flush each mebibyte the head yields.** No spill, so 0.7.0's cost plus 120 bytes at every
  size and 2.2 MB at 67 MB where 0.7.0 held the head whole (17.7 MB); a refusal before the first flush
  points the writer away so disposing it writes nothing, and so does a success, where disposing would
  flush the caller's stream synchronously. A read-only stream is refused by that writer, and the
  non-finite, block and id checks run after it, in `Save(Stream)` and `SaveAsync` alike, as 0.8.0
  refused a read-only stream ahead of a non-finite vector — an order #1214, unreleased, had inverted.
- **A read the open segment holds takes one copy and no loop** (#1636).
- **`SegmentChain`'s constructor takes one length**: nothing doubled since #1632.
- **The #1629 spec's misplaced band is corrected in place**, the bullet saying it was.
- **The tests hold every band sync and async**, at 81,920- and 65,536-byte reads, the seekable
  baseline warmed too; the test stream answers `ReadAsync` at once, as Stream's own hops threads and
  a per-thread allocation count cannot follow it.
