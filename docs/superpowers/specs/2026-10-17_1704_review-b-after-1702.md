# The Review B after #1702: F at an infinite f, small-argument densities, degenerate samples and two re-rankings

**Issues:** [#1704](https://github.com/CyrilB1531/lodestar/issues/1704) to
[#1718](https://github.com/CyrilB1531/lodestar/issues/1718).
**Status:** written with the work, 2026-10-17.
**Date:** 2026-10-17.

## The problem

The Review B after #1702, on `main` at `881e9415`, ran Lodestar.Stats against its pre-merge `9c194567`,
scipy 1.18.1 and exact decimal sums, on both target frameworks. Fifteen findings were not cosmetic:

- one regression from #1702: `FisherSnedecorIsf` threw at a subnormal numerator df and `p` near `1e-310`,
  where `9c194567` answered `+∞` (#1704), because the check of that answer evaluated the tail at `f = +∞`,
  which threw wherever `dfd / dfn` overflows (#1705);
- degenerate samples answered unlike scipy: a constant Shapiro-Wilk sample whose mean rounds read
  `W = 0` (#1706), a Fligner group with an infinite centre answered finite values (#1707), negative
  counts and overflowing marginals reached the incomplete gamma, which refused them under its own `x`
  (#1709, #1710), and groups of one value each made `OneWayAnova` throw (#1712);
- the chi-squared and F densities formed `x^a` before dividing by `x`, reading 0 at `1e-70` (#1708);
- the netstandard2.0 Fisher transform read `atanh(1e-9)` 8e-8 off (#1711);
- five documentation errors (#1712 to #1716);
- `Spearman.Matrix` re-ranked every pair once one `NaN` was present under `omit`, and `Friedman.Test`
  allocated three arrays per block (#1717, #1718).

## Decisions

- **f = +∞ is the support's end.** The F tails answer `0` and `1` there before splitting `f`, and
  `FisherSplit` takes `dfd / f / dfn` where `dfd / dfn / f` overflows or forms `∞/∞`, which fixes
  both #1705 and, through it, #1704.
- **The densities keep main's form, and fall back only where it loses its digits.** Below `x = 2` the
  chi-squared density of a shape in `[0.1, 10)` whose prefactor is below `1e-30`, an exponent past −69, takes `x^(a−1)` by
  `Math.Pow` times one exponential of `−x/2 − a ln 2 − log Γ(a)`, which also takes the 2e-13 the exponent of
  −690 cost at `1e-62`; from a shape of 10 only a prefactor below the smallest normal double goes to logs; below 0.1 a subnormal
  `x` is not halved. F's front below the smallest normal double takes `y^(a−1)` by `Math.Pow` in two halves
  around one exponential of the rest, from `log y` by way of `1 − y` past `y = ½`, or logs where the halves leave the normal doubles, below a shape of 0.1, or past
  shapes of 1000 on both sides, where `log B` by log-gammas cancels against the powers. Three first cuts
  failed the Review A: one at `1e-20` answered `+∞` at `x = 5e-324` and 0 for F outside a second shape of
  `[0.1, 10)`; one that took F's power form below `y = ½` rounded `1 − y` first and read
  `FisherSnedecorPdf(1, 1, 1e14)` 4e-4 off; one with the power whole read `(1e-33, 20, 10000)` 3.6e-3 off
  and stepped 230-fold at shapes of 1e15; and a chi-squared fallback keyed on the prefactor alone took the upper tail
  too, `inf × 0` reading `ChiSquaredPdf(1e100, 10)` NaN; and an F floor at `1e-290` sent normal fronts to the
  fallback, `FisherSnedecorPdf(0.1, 1e14, 200)` 8e-5 off. Against 80- and 90-digit sums the fallbacks hold 2e-15 to
  1.5e-14 while their powers stay normal, and 1e-12 past; elsewhere the densities are main's to the bit,
  and scipy's `f.pdf` is 4e-14 off at `f = 1e-300`. The fallbacks sit out of line: inside, they cost the
  ordinary density 4%; outside, the chi-squared density is level with main's and the F one 3 to 5% slower,
  80 against 82 to 84 ns at (1.5, 4, 9).
- **Degenerate samples follow each test's defining document, and `NaN` where it is silent**
  ([decision 0010](../../decisions/0010-each-algorithm-follows-its-defining-document.md), which this
  work wrote at the maintainer's word). Shapiro-Wilk follows Royston's AS R94: it refuses fewer than
  three values and a range below `1e-19`, divides by an extreme range, computes past 5000 values, and shifts the sample by the value at
  its middle position, as scipy's `shapiro` does (gh-15777), so three 0.1 are a zero range and no
  longer read `W = 0`; at three values W is clipped to its bound of 3/4 and p is `1 − 6/π acos(√W)`.
  Where the document is silent and the statistic undefined, the answer is `NaN`, given or left by
  omission: an empty sample in the t-tests, Mann-Whitney, Kolmogorov-Smirnov and Wilcoxon, a null or
  empty group in the five k-sample tests, groups of one value in `OneWayAnova`, a group of one in
  Bartlett, no block in Friedman, a fully tied pool in Kruskal-Wallis, and no category in the
  chi-squared goodness of fit (`0`, `NaN`), a zero marginal in the contingency test and a zero expectation
  no count meets in the goodness of fit (where one does, `+∞` and `p = 0`, as scipy's),
  fewer than two pairs in Pearson, an empty sample in Anderson-Darling and an empty sample or a pool
  of one distinct value in its k-sample form; the chi-squared and F densities answer their limit,
  zero, at `+∞`. Every option outside its enumeration — the `NanPolicy` included — is refused on every
  input, these answers included, but last: a member refuses the option once its input has met every refusal of its own — on its way out
  where a refusal is met inside the computation — so every
  refusal its input meets inside the computation — the alternative where its p-value is read, an exact table's size, a
  sample omission left too short — keeps its turn (`AndersonDarling.KSample`'s variant aside, which main refused
  before the samples and still is), and an undefined policy omits as every one but `Propagate` and
  `Raise` always has. Two first cuts refused the options up front and the policy before them, which reordered those
  refusals; a grid of every member against main showed it.
- **A normal approximation without variance answers a `NaN` p-value, as scipy's does.** Mann-Whitney's continuity
  correction carries the deviation's sign, none at zero, so a pool tied throughout reads 0/0 two-sided, and 1 one-sided
  where the correction is applied; Wilcoxon's z is 0/0 before its correction on every side. Every difference
  zero under Wilcox's zero method answers `(0, 1)` from the exact and permutation nulls and `(0, NaN)` from the normal
  one, `Auto` choosing past 13 values as scipy's does; Kolmogorov-Smirnov's one value against one, two-sided and
  asymptotic, rounds its law's size to none, `NaN`. Fligner's
  deviations that all tie are 0/0 too, `NaN`, where scipy's rounding answers `NaN` or a finite statistic by the groups.
  An empty group or a propagated `NaN` answers `NaN` in Levene and Fligner whatever the trim's size (a `NaN` proportion aside, which is refused), as
  scipy's do; an empty p-value family adjusts to
  an empty one, as `false_discovery_control([])`; `Binomial.Test` at `int.MaxValue` trials no longer overflows to
  `p = 1` for an impossible count, and its interval at no trial keeps the limit a one-sided test fixes. A contingency table of one row or one column with no
  zero total answers `(0, 1)` on no degree of freedom; a Pearson interval below two pairs keeps a one-sided test's fixed bound. Fligner answers `NaN` where a deviation is `inf − inf`, and ranks the infinite
  deviations of a centre that only overflowed, as scipy does; the chi-squared tests answer a `NaN`
  p-value for a negative or `NaN` statistic. A first cut aligned every degenerate answer with scipy
  test by test, which drifted and contradicted decision 0007; another kept the package's refusals
  and documented them; the maintainer settled it as 0010 states.
- **The Fisher transform keeps `Math.Atanh` on net10.0**, glibc's as numpy's arctanh is; netstandard2.0
  takes `(log1p(r) − log1p(−r)) / 2`, within two ulps of it. Taking that form on both made them
  bit-identical but moved net10.0 off scipy's bits, which the Review A found.
- **Complete pairs reuse their columns' ranks.** Under `omit`, only a pair holding a `NaN` is re-ranked;
  a first cut followed scipy's `mstats` path there (infinities masked, a constant sample read as rho 0,
  one pair refused), which decision 0010 rejects: omission answers as the same pairs passed directly, but for the diagonal's `(1, 0)`,
  main's, for every column once a `NaN` is omitted;
  the others answer `Spearman.Test`'s value bit for bit, which a test pins. 300 variables × 200 rows with
  one `NaN`: 393 ms and 663 MB on `main`, 24 ms and 9 MB here.
- **Friedman ranks every block in one set of scratch arrays**, through an overload of
  `Ranks.AverageWithTies` that takes them: 3 × 1e6 went from 38 ms and 136 MB to 24.6 ms and 0.3 KB.
- **Cosmetics carried:** `NanFilter` returns the caller's sample, or group, under `Raise`, and under
  `Omit` where it holds no `NaN`, rather than a copy (a Welch test of 200 went from 1.1 µs and 3.3 KB to 0.93 µs and 64 B
  under `Raise`, and from 1.47 µs and 6.6 KB to 0.92 µs under `Omit`); `Spearman.Matrix`'s unreachable branches
  are gone; `BenjaminiYekutieli` validates once;
  `Ranks.TieCorrection` and `HasTies`, with no production caller, moved to the tests as their reference.

## Measured

AMD Ryzen 7 8700G, .NET 10.0.12, BenchmarkDotNet 0.14.0 (warm-up 3, ten iterations), A/B/A/B against
`main` at `881e9415` with arguments in fields, under the machine lock, 2026-10-17. The Pearson interval and
the chi-squared density are level; Shapiro-Wilk at 500 costs 2% for its shift, the F density 3 to 5%.
The first Review A round's fixes, against `ee78dd08`, A/B/A/B at 200 values: every touched path level or faster
(Welch under `omit` with a `NaN` 1.18 to 1.15 µs and 3.3 to 1.6 KB, Bartlett under `omit` 5.0 to 4.7 µs, Shapiro-Wilk
under `omit` 8.1 to 6.5 KB), but Mann-Whitney's asymptotic test, which BenchmarkDotNet read 8% slower, 3.05 against 3.31
µs, from 20 to 2000 values. Off dynamic PGO the two are level, 3.60 and 3.56 µs; in a plain loop of 200,000 calls
they are level too, 3.03 to 3.18 µs on each side: the gap is a PGO choice inside the harness's own method, not the
test's, and no reshaping of `MannWhitney.Test` — options at each return, before the ranks, or the ranks out of line — moved it.
