# The Review B after #1741: the incomplete beta to its last bit, and the F and Student quantiles on it

**Issues:** [#1742](https://github.com/CyrilB1531/lodestar/issues/1742),
[#1743](https://github.com/CyrilB1531/lodestar/issues/1743),
[#1745](https://github.com/CyrilB1531/lodestar/issues/1745) and
[#1746](https://github.com/CyrilB1531/lodestar/issues/1746).
**Status:** written with the work, 2026-10-26.
**Date:** 2026-10-25 to 2026-10-28.

## The problem

Below F dfs of about `1e-8` the continued fraction's two small-shape sides each round `b/(a + b)` their own way, so
the F tail fell by an ulp or three as `f` grew across the mode (#1743), and the quantile, an inversion stopping within
`RootTolerance` on a tail that moves by a few ulps over the whole support, read `1.0000007` for `0` one ulp below the
flat tail. The same held at every shape (#1745): a tail rises by about `a ε` of itself an ulp of `f`, and the double
forms carried one to hundreds of ulps of noise, so over 196 pairs of dfs from `1e-11` to 100 and 3.9 million steps of
`f` the F tails fell 123,794 times, scipy's 24,392. Elsewhere an inversion that overflowed past `double.MaxValue` came
back `+∞` unchecked, so `Isf(Sf(MaxValue, 0.5, 0.5), 0.5, 0.5)` read `+∞` (#1746).

## Decisions

- **The contract is 8 ulps** (Cyril, 2026-10-29, after pass 62). Tails and densities lie within 8 ulps of their exact
  value, and a quantile's tail within 8 ulps of its target, or the answer next to the crossing where one ulp of the statistic moves the tail further, outside the double forms the next entries keep (an F shape
  sum past `1e15`, a Student df past `1e20`, any harmonic shape past 1000, Temme's region included, Cyril, 2026-10-30;
  the F density at a df below `2⁻¹⁰²¹`, lifted, main's double forms, up to some 600 ulps, pass 69): past Algorithm 708's certified 14 to 15 digits (DiDonato and
  Morris, 1992; Brown and Levy, 1994), which scipy's and Boost's forms do not reach either. The last bit and the
  smallest double reaching the target are the method's aim, not its promise; a review reports a gap past 8 ulps, a
  regression against main, or a wrong value (0, ∞ or NaN for a finite one), and nothing finer.
- **The function is unchanged; the method is the same in 106 bits.** Each form of `I_x(a, b)` the double code takes,
  the fraction's prefactor `x^a y^b / (a B)`, the fraction, the small-shape series, the large-shape expansion and its
  incomplete gamma, and Temme's `erfc`, is evaluated in double-double (Dekker, 1971) and rounded once. Correctly
  rounded tails are monotone, and those are, to the last bit, on every grid measured. A double-double type carries
  `log`, `exp`, `expm1`, `log Γ` and `log B` to some 1e-30, by table reduction and Stirling's series past 20.
- **The point is never rounded before the tail.** F's `d₁f/(d₁f + d₂)` and Student's `df/(df + t²)` are formed to
  106 bits and passed whole: rounded to a double, x carried `b ε/2` of the tail, hundreds of ulps at shapes in the
  hundreds, and its numerator and denominator, rounded apart, made x itself step back as `f` rose.
- **Where 106 bits do not hold, or cost past scipy's time, the double forms stay.** Temme's exponent past shapes summing
  to `1e15`, where the point's `1e-32` times the shapes exceeds an ulp; the large-shape expansion where its point is
  exactly one; and the fraction past a harmonic shape `ab/(a + b)` of 1000, whose terms grow as its square root, 30 µs in double-double at dfs of `1.9e5`:
  there an ulp of `f` moves the tail by tens of ulps, past the double fraction's rounding. The large-shape expansion's
  incomplete gamma takes Temme's form in double-double from a shape of 100, the series' first `10 − 2k` terms of each
  `c_k` from their exact rationals and the rest in double, under `1e-27` of the sum; below 100 its series or fraction.
- **The first order's reach is relative to the smaller share**, `7.5e-9 √(min(a, b)/(a + b))`: its `2⁻⁵⁵` was absolute,
  8 ulps of a share of 0.016, and the tail stepped up 2 where the fraction took over.
- **The tiny-shape first order stays.** Where `m = max(a, b)(1 + |log x| + |log y|)` is below `7.5e-9`,
  `I_x(a, b) = b/(a + b) + ab/(a + b) log(x/y)` within about `0.5 m²`, 2.35e-17 at worst over 2,689 points; the F
  quantile there searches the doubles from that form's root, below dfs of `6.2e-9`.
- **A quantile is the smallest double whose lower tail before its last rounding reaches the target, an inverse tail
  the smallest whose upper tail falls to it** (Cyril, 2026-10-28, pass 37), judged on the smaller tail, one less a target
  past one half, which is exact there: the root within an ulp. Judged on the rounded tail, the generalized inverse, the
  answer was the edge of a run of doubles sharing a tail value, 1,284 ulps off at `StudentIsf(0.4999, 1000)` and half the
  root at `p = 0.49999999999999994`, where main and scipy held an ulp; `Q(F(x)) ≤ x` no longer holds to the ulp; Student's mirror takes the other side's largest double still at the target, the double below the first
  strictly past it. A subnormal target keeps the inversion's root where its tail is subnormal and not 0, as main and scipy: the smallest double reaching it
  starts a run of doubles whose search took 199 µs at `FisherSnedecorIsf(5e-324, 1e-300, 2000)`, against scipy's 26 µs; kept, 4.5 µs. The median of equal dfs is one, the tails at one exactly one half now. The inversion, now by the double forms, which an inversion's Newton steps need no more than, lands
  within a few ulps; Newton steps on the exact tail before its last rounding, aimed at the target, and a search of the
  doubles within 128 ulps settle it. Aimed at the target on the rounded tail, the steps
  moved nothing inside a run of doubles sharing a tail value, hundreds of ulps of `t` wide near one half at every df,
  and the search's window missed the run's start. Where the tail falls back to a double form, as at a df past `1e15`
  and from the chi-squared limit, the steps aim at the target, and where no crossing lies within 128 ulps the doubles
  are searched from the root however far: kept, that root read 1,522 ulps past the crossing at dfs `(0.001, 1e6)`. Each step is over the statistic times the density, which stays a double where the density
  underflows near the largest double.
- **A quantile's two or three exact tails share their fraction's terms.** The terms apart from the point, each
  denominator multiplying its step instead of dividing its numerator, are kept per thread for the last shapes, and the
  search takes the root's test as read: F's inverse tail at dfs (4000, 4000) took 30 µs, past scipy's 26, and takes 19.
- **A quantile of `+∞` whose smaller tail at `MaxValue` reaches the target is a double**, searched as any other.
- **At the largest double the rounded tail judges** (Cyril, 2026-10-29). A root past every double is `±∞`, unless
  at the largest double of its sign, rounded as `Sf` or `Cdf` round it, the caller's tail is the target, or the smaller
  tail the smaller of the target and one less it: that double then. Either, so every round trip holds (Cyril,
  2026-10-29): on the smaller alone `Isf(Sf(MaxValue))` read `+∞` where `Sf` there passes one half, in a quarter of
  random dfs; the caller's side near one rounds 1e-16 wide, so `Q(1 − p) = −Q(p)` can part within 1e-16 of one. The
  end's tail rounds onto the target from short of it, so on the exact tail alone `Isf(Sf(MaxValue, 0.5, 0.5), 0.5, 0.5)` and
  `StudentIsf(StudentSf(MaxValue, 0.05), 0.05)` stayed `±∞`. Equality, not reaching: on the lower side `-MaxValue`'s
  tail reaches every target below it, whose root still lies past the doubles (#1690). Only an infinite answer reads
  that tail, 0.6 µs: `FisherSnedecorIsf(0.01, 3e-9, 4e-9)` takes 3.2 µs, scipy's 25.
- **F's chi-squared limit to 106 bits** (Cyril, 2026-10-29). Where a half of the split is subnormal, `P(s, z)` is the
  double-double incomplete gamma at `z = o y` formed to 106 bits: as a double it fell 34 ulps between neighbouring `f`
  at dfs `(30, 1e307)`, and 2 where it met the 106-bit forms at `2⁻¹⁰²²`, dfs `(0.3, 1e300)`. `FisherSnedecorCdf(0.05,
  1, 1e307)` takes 1.5 µs, scipy's 20, and the quantile there 6.4, scipy's 25.5.
- **Left as is.** Past a harmonic shape of 1000, below Temme's shapes of `1e5`, the tail is main's double forms, on the 106-bit split's high part:
  the double fraction steps some 1e4 ulps between runs of `f` at dfs `(165557, 8597663)`, which a 106-bit front only
  made dearer, and the fraction to 106 bits took 20 to 60 µs. Temme's
  region keeps its own truncation, its remainder two terms in double: far in the tails some 10 to 200 ulps of 90-digit
  sums, either way of `main`'s, `FisherSnedecorSf(1.1131254103811055, 207883.79, 9257558.4)` 213 (pass 67); smooth in
  `f`, it causes no fall, and lies outside the contract. Tried and dropped: the caller's side in the search (near one its ulps are 1e-4 of the tail, and
  `f.ppf(1 − 1e-12, 5, 5)` read 2.2e-5 off), a Newton step of up to `1e-12` (it left the window at dfs (3e235, 3e139)),
  and Lentz's three divisions a term in the fraction, a forward recurrence taking 17% less.

## Settled

Raised by Review A and decided; each is reopened only by a new fact, an input it fails or a cost behind scipy.

- **The cost of 106 bits.** The tails take 3 to 28 times main's time, the F density 5, Student's 4 to 18, about
  0.4 µs to scipy's 47, Student's quantile up to 15.5 µs at large df: the bar is the Python reference's, scipy's here, and every point measured stays ahead
  of it, the worst 23 µs for 27. Going back to the double forms would give back the falls this fixes.
- **Double and double-double forms side by side.** The double forms serve the inversions' Newton steps and Pearson's,
  Spearman's and the binomial test's p-values (Spearman's matrix took 488 ms at 1,000 variables in 106 bits, scipy's
  160, pass 67), which invert no quantile and keep main's values (Cyril, 2026-10-27); one form
  would put the 106 bits' cost on them. The one exception is I_½(a, a), one half on both paths: `Binomial.Test(0, 1,
  0.5)` reads 1 where main read 0.9999999999999982 (pass 29).
- **A subnormal target keeps the inversion's root** where that root's tail is subnormal too, as main and scipy: below
  a subnormal's granule the exact tail tells nothing, and searching for a run's end cost 199 µs (Cyril, 2026-10-27).
  Not a tail of 0, a root short of a law narrower than an ulp: `FisherSnedecorQuantile(1e-310, 1e40, 1e41)` kept
  `1 − 2⁻⁵³` for 1 (#1746's Review A).
- **The median of equal dfs is one**, the tails there exactly one half, so the quantiles stay in order up to a
  harmonic shape of 1000. Past it, where the root is kept, the double fraction's tail beside one is some 90 ulps off
  one half at dfs `(1e4, 1e4)`, and the quantiles either side of `p = 0.5` cross 1 (pass 43).
- **The fraction's terms are kept per thread**, at most 4,096 pairs, about 128 KB a thread, for the last shapes: built
  per tail, they took F's quantile at dfs (4000, 4000) to 30 µs, past scipy's 26. A search straddling the mode swaps
  the shapes and rebuilds them, the worst 14 µs over p from 0.45 to 0.5 at dfs near (2000, 2000), scipy's 26.
- **F's split is formed to 106 bits for every reader**, the density's and the Newton steps' included, so each side
  moves with `f`; `FisherTail` reads `TakesLimit` from those sides, and building them first costs some 30 ns there.
- **Past a harmonic shape of 1000, below Temme's shapes, the F quantile answers the inversion's root**, as main
  (Cyril, 2026-10-27): its search on the stepping double tail took 59 µs at dfs `(165557, 8597663)`, scipy's 29. Of
  the review grid's 346 answers there 162 fall short of the target, main's 206; scipy's `f.ppf` and `f.isf` answer
  `+∞` or nothing for 132 of them and fall short on 112 of their 214 finite answers, finite here wherever scipy's are.
- **Student's closed forms stay unsettled**, the normal limit from df `1e20` and the first order to `1e-12`, as the
  reference pages say.
- **Where the tail moves by less than an ulp of the target over `2²⁰` doubles of f, or is a lifted df's double form,
  the F quantile answers the root** unless the crossing is at the Newton steps' landing, the root or a neighbour of
  either (Cyril, 2026-10-28, pass 30; the root's neighbour from pass 47): the search took some 40 tails, 116 to 168 µs at dfs near `5e-9` and 47 to 85 µs at a lifted df and a
  target near `2⁻¹⁰²²`, for scipy's 26; now 8 to 13 µs. Below `6.2e-9` the first order's guess is settled as the
  inversion's root, 160 µs to 12.
- **The gamma tail's fraction past a + 1 at shapes from 0.1 to 1 stays**: `FisherSnedecorIsf(0.975, 1e300, 0.25)` takes
  18.7 µs warmed under the machine lock, scipy's `f.isf` 29.7 µs on the same machine, and one `FisherSnedecorSf(0.11,
  1e300, 0.25)` 1.7 µs; the series there moved neither (pass 36).
- **Student's gamma limit at shape ½ keeps its series and fraction** rather than `DoubleDouble.ErfcOfSquareRoot`, which
  runs a series or fraction of its own, so saves nothing, and holds `1e-25` of itself against the tail's 106 bits
  (pass 31).
- **Past `ExactExponentShapes` the F density's front stays 106-bit**, not the double form: from the split below a shape
  of 10 (pass 32: from the rounded point `FisherSnedecorPdf(245, 3.7, 1e300)` read 4.2e-14 off), Stirling's from the
  rounded point past it; over the grid's 31 densities at a df of `1e300` within `1.1e-15` of the chi-squared limit, the
  double form up to `1.6e-13` (pass 27).
- **`FisherTarget.Front` forms its split again** rather than reuse the exact tail's: holding it would take
  `FisherTarget` past the four fields the JIT promotes, which cost 8% (#1690), for one quotient per Newton step (pass 40).
- **A lifted tail rounds from its high part scaled back**, not `Value.Rounded`: the low part, rounded onto the
  subnormals first, moved a tie, and `FisherSnedecorSf(3.140388479691043e204, 2, 3)` read an ulp off (pass 40).
- **netstandard2.0's 106-bit products are Dekker's**, that target having no fused multiply-add: some 1.7 times
  net10's, `FisherSnedecorIsf(1e-10, 1e-9, 1000)` 39 µs to scipy's 27 where that asset runs on .NET 10, which loads
  the net10.0 one instead. One test before the split, not six guards, took 6 to 18% off (pass 45).
- **At a tie, `p = ½` at unequal dfs, the inversion judges the lower tail** and the settle the caller's: the targets
  are equal, and the root's tail is read twice at that one `p`. One tie rule threaded through would take
  `FisherFromTail` past seven parameters (pass 48).
- **The F density forms its 106-bit front before its underflow test**, and recomputes by the double forms where it
  underflowed: predicting the underflow costs a second form on every call, and the underflow is the rare case (pass 53).
- **The support's ends keep their judges where they are read**: the start's, the window's, the far search's and the
  limit's each judge the side and the target the inversion holds there. One step answering 0, ε, `MaxValue` or `+∞`
  would rewrite paths fifty passes settled, for no input they answer wrongly; the grid's ends and refusal classes are
  their check (pass 54).
- **A lifted `ExactTail` scales back on each read of `Value`**: `Less`, the searches' test, reads the held value and
  scales the target, so a probe scales nothing; a cached copy would add a field to a struct passed by value (pass 54).
- **`WalkFromMean` gallops** and keeps no first crossing a one-ulp walk would: it runs only for a law whose relative
  width is under 4 ulps, where each ulp moves the tail by a quarter of a standard deviation or more, a change no tail's
  noise of hundreds of ulps of itself can undo, so no target meets the tail twice there (#1746's Review A, pass 57).
- **`TinyLogScaledBeta` keeps no cache**: keyed on its last pair as `DoubleDouble.LogBeta` is, it took
  `FisherSnedecorQuantile(0.3, 1e-14, 5)` from 3.2 to 3.5 µs, its pairs not repeating within a quantile (pass 61).
- **`ExactRegularizedGamma` forms its prefactor on every path**, Temme's and the small shape's too, which the chi-squared
  limit discards: about 0.1 µs of its 1.5, scipy's 20; skipping it pushed the method past Sonar's complexity bound (pass
  65).
- **Past a harmonic shape of 1000, below Temme's shapes, the double forms read the 106-bit split's high part**, not main's
  split: single tails move by up to thousands of ulps either way of main's, the noise of a one-ulp move of the point
  times slopes of 1e4 ulps, outside the 8-ulp contract (pass 65).
- **Timings read beside another load are not costs**: pass 60's 30 to 66 µs for Student and F quantiles at dfs in the
  hundreds to thousands ran beside a process at 185% CPU; idle, under the machine lock, the same calls take 4.3 to
  17.2 µs, scipy's 18.4 to 30 (2026-10-29), and `performance.md` gives the narrower margin there.
- **`bench/README.md`'s OLS-against-Accord paragraph changes here**, with the Regression page this PR re-measured: it
  said this package loses at 10,000 rows, where the new run has it 10× ahead; the pull request names it (pass 53).

## Measured

Falls between neighbouring doubles of the statistic, Lodestar and scipy on the same points: over 196 pairs of F dfs
from `1e-11` to 100 and 3.9 million steps, 0 and 24,392; over 100 pairs from `1e-320` to `1e8`, 2 and 26,200; over
twelve Student dfs from `1e-11` to `1e4`, 0 and 1,226. Against 90-digit sums over 6,000 random F tails, dfs from
`1e-6` to `1e4`, the mean error falls from 6.97 ulps to 0.0065 and the worst from 1,418 to 1, none further than
`main`; past dfs of `2e5`, from 7.5 to 0.84 and from 148 to 17.
