**Issues:** [#1728](https://github.com/CyrilB1531/lodestar/issues/1728) and

**Status:** written with the work, 2026-10-18.

## The problem

The Review B after #1727, on `main` at `c4a19d86`, ran Lodestar.Stats against its pre-merge `6db2890d`, scipy 1.18.1
and 1000-digit sums. Two findings were not cosmetic:

- the F row of `docs/equivalence.md` still said identical where #1727 had made scipy the one off, at a subnormal
  `d₁f` (#1728);
- `FisherSnedecorSf(1e-318, 20, 1e-320)` read `0.999943` where the exact tail, one less `2.39e-320`, rounds to one (#1729).

Two were cosmetic: `FisherSplit` fell back to the quantised `d₁f` where `f/d₂` overflowed, and its remarks did not
name the subnormal branch.

## Decisions

- **The fraction's first term is formed without its `a`** where `a (a + b) x` is subnormal. Lentz's first numerator
  is `−a (a + b) x / (a (a + 1))`, and those products quantised there; below the mode, where the fraction is read,
  `a (a + 1)` is the larger, so it is normal wherever the first is. The term is taken out of the loop and formed as
  `−(a + b) x / (a + 1)` there, and as before elsewhere. Cancelling `a` everywhere was tried: it moved 811 of 139,000
  random F and t values by up to 1e-13, and of 60 such F tails 38 came nearer a 1000-digit sum and 22 went further, so
  the old form keeps its bits.
- **`FisherSplit` takes `(d₁/d₂) f` where `f/d₂` overflows** as well as where it is subnormal; #1727's spec says that
  quotient cannot overflow, and it can. There both dfs are below `2.5e-293` and each tail is fixed by the shapes' ratio
  rather than by y, so no public member moves; the split itself is tested, 5.4e-5 off before. It takes the odds where
  `d₁f` underflows to zero too: `(8.4e-323, 0.0036, 3.8e-317)` read y = 0 for 7.8e-9, the density 1.4e-11 off.
- **A df whose half is not a double enters at its exact half, lifted.** Below `2⁻¹⁰²¹` an odd multiple of the smallest
  double has no half among the doubles, and `FisherSnedecorCdf(1e-5, 1e-310, 1.5e-323)`, about `d₂/d₁`, read
  `1.98e-13` for `1.48e-13`; scipy answers `0`. A shape that small makes the density and the small tail that shape
  times a function of the statistic, and the other tail one less it; with both F shapes that small the tails are
  `b/(a + b)` and `a/(a + b)` and the density `ab/(a + b)` times one. So such a df is lifted by `2^200`, exact, the
  law taken at the lifted shapes, whose halves are exact and still below `2^-821`, where it keeps that degree, and the
  answer of degree one dropped by `2^-200` once. An F df below `2^-700` beside a lifted one is lifted with it, so
  their ratio stands; above it a lone lifted shape is under `2^-121` of the other. The primitives that take a df lift
  it through `ExactHalf`, which holds the lift, the side of degree one, the lifted target and the drop, and nothing
  else halves a df: `Gamma.ChiSquaredTail` and `ChiSquaredTailBetween`, the densities, Beta's F tails, which one-way
  ANOVA and the chi-squared tests call as the public members do, and the inverses, `ChiSquaredRoot` and
  `FisherQuantile`, moved out of `Distributions` for it, which solve the lifted law at the targets lifted where of
  degree one, a lower chi-squared root as the upper one at one less the target, judging the root against the targets
  themselves. So the refusal of a df of `5e-324`, whose half rounds to zero, goes: lifted, it is a df like the others.
