# The Review B after #1722: two NaN scans, a quantised F argument and a stale equivalence row

**Issues:** [#1723](https://github.com/CyrilB1531/lodestar/issues/1723) to
[#1726](https://github.com/CyrilB1531/lodestar/issues/1726).
**Status:** written with the work, 2026-10-17.
**Date:** 2026-10-17.

## The problem

The Review B after #1722, on `main` at `6db2890d`, ran Lodestar.Stats against its pre-merge `4f4ada76`, scipy 1.18.1
and 80-digit sums, on both target frameworks. Four findings were not cosmetic:

- `Levene.Test` scanned every value for a `NaN` on every call, 10 to 17% slower (#1723);
- `Fligner.Test`'s `NaN` flag and its two passes for all-tied deviations cost 6 to 8% (#1724);
- `FisherSnedecorPdf` read a subnormal `d₁f`, quantised, 3 to 8% off at a tiny denominator df (#1725);
- the `f_oneway` row of `docs/equivalence.md` named underflow alone where scipy's sums of squares also cancel (#1726).

## Decisions

- **Levene scans for a `NaN` only where the trim would be read first**: under `Center.Trimmed` and `Propagate`. Every
  other centre under `Propagate` reaches `(NaN, NaN)` through its deviations, as before #1722.
- **Fligner reads its two undefined cases off what it already has.** Past its scan for a propagated `NaN`, a deviation
  is `NaN` only where a centre is `NaN`,
  or infinite beside an equal value, which the k centres show; every deviation tied is one tie group of all N, whose
  term `N³ − N` the ranking already sums. Its scan for a propagated `NaN`, main's, runs under `Propagate` alone.
- **`FisherSplit` takes y from `r = d₁f/d₂`** where `d₁f` is subnormal and r a normal double, formed as `d₁ (f/d₂)` so
  it cannot overflow, or as `(d₁/d₂) f` where `f/d₂` is subnormal itself: `y = r/(1 + r)` and
  `1 − y = 1/(1 + r)`, for the density, both tails and the quantiles at once. A `d₁f` that underflowed to zero keeps
  `y = 0`, whose limit held `(1e-300, 1e-300, 1e-300)` to 4e-15, where the corrected y read 9.4e-14. A first cut took the whole density from
  logs; it moved `(5e-324, 2, 1e-300)` from 1 to `1 − 1.1e-13`, the logs of `B(a, b)` and f cancelling, which the grid
  showed. A second fixed y in the density alone, as `(d₁/d₂) f`: the quotient overflowed at `d₂ = 1e-308`, `(1e-323, 3.7,
  1e-308)` stayed 4.6% off, and the lower tail kept its 8% at `(1e-323, 0.3, 1e-300)`, which the Review A found.
- **The f_oneway row** states where the two part: scipy's sums of squares cancel on a group offset by `1e15` beside a
  constant one, with the executed values.
- **One `log(1 − x)`:** `BinomialTail`, `BetaQuantile` and `Normal` take it from `Beta.LogComplement` rather than three
  copies of their own. Where `1 − x ≤ ½`, that is `Math.Log(1 − x)` rather than the series, which moved 13 of 3,000 random
  proportion intervals and 109 of 20,000 random F quantiles, by up to 63 and 16 ulps, no nearer scipy and no further
  overall.
