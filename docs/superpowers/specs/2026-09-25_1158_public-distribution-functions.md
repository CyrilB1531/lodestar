# The four laws a caller computes with, published whole

**Issue:** [#1158](https://github.com/CyrilB1531/lodestar/issues/1158).
**Status:** written before the work, 2026-09-25.
**Date:** 2026-09-25.

## The problem

`Distributions` publishes five members, each because one sibling package asked:
`StudentSf`, `StudentQuantile`, `FisherSf`, `ChiSquaredSf` and `NormalQuantile`.
`docs/equivalence.md` records the rest as *no counterpart*: `t.cdf`, `t.isf`, `f.cdf`,
`norm.*`, `chi2.*`. A caller writing their own test, interval or power calculation starts from
exactly those, and has to leave the package to get them. The numerics already exist under
`src/Lodestar.Stats/Internal/` except one: nothing inverts the incomplete gamma, which the
chi-squared quantiles need.

## Decisions

1. **Twenty members, the five that exist among them: pdf, cdf, sf, quantile and inverse sf for
   each of the standard normal, Student's t, F and chi-squared laws.** They are added to
   `Distributions` under the names it already uses: `<Law><Function>` with `Normal`, `Student`,
   `Fisher` and `ChiSquared`, and `Pdf`, `Cdf`, `Sf`, `Quantile` and `Isf`. `Quantile` is
   scipy's `ppf`, the name the two existing quantiles already carry. Rejected: one static class
   per law (`StudentT.Cdf`). That would rename five published members, or leave two spellings
   side by side.
2. **The normal law is the standard one**, as `NormalQuantile` already is. `loc` and `scale`
   are one multiplication and one addition away, and the reference page says so.
3. **The quantiles answer scipy's endpoints instead of refusing them.** `ppf(0)` and `ppf(1)` are
   the support's ends: `-∞` and `+∞` for the normal and t laws, `0` and `+∞` for F and
   chi-squared. `isf` is the mirror. `StudentQuantile` and `NormalQuantile` refused `0` and `1`
   until now; they now answer them, **a behaviour change** the changelog records. A probability
   outside `[0, 1]` or `NaN`, and a non-positive or `NaN` degrees-of-freedom, stay refused with
   `ArgumentOutOfRangeException`, which is how every entry point of this package treats an
   argument that has no meaning. Where scipy returns `nan` instead, `docs/equivalence.md` says so.
4. **A `NaN` statistic propagates**: `pdf`, `cdf` and `sf` of `NaN` are `NaN`, as scipy's are.
5. **No new data type**, so nothing is added to `Lodestar.Abstractions`: every member is a
   static method on the existing `Distributions` class.

## The numerics

| law | pdf | cdf / sf | quantile / isf |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| normal | `exp(-z²/2)/√(2π)` | `Normal.Sf`, and its mirror for the cdf | `Normal.Quantile` (the upper-tail inverse) and its negation |
| Student t | log-density from `Gamma.LogGamma`, `log1p(t²/ν)` | `Beta.StudentSf` and its mirror | `Beta.StudentQuantile` and its negation |
| F | log-density from `Gamma.LogGamma` and `log1p` | the regularized incomplete beta on the side that keeps the smaller probability | `BetaQuantile.Invert` on the side that keeps the smaller probability |
| chi-squared | log-density from `Gamma.LogGamma` | `Gamma.RegularizedP` and `RegularizedQ` | **new**: `GammaQuantile.Invert`, Newton on `log P` or `log Q` against `log x` in a growing bracket, seeded by Wilson-Hilferty, solving whichever of `p` and `1 − p` is smaller as `BetaQuantile` does |

Each tail is computed on the side that keeps full relative precision. A cdf is never `1 − sf`,
which loses every digit once the cdf is small.

## What the review found

The code review of the pull request found four defects in the F functions, and fixing them found
two more underneath, all corrected in the same pull request:

- **`FisherCdf(+∞)` threw and `FisherPdf(+∞)` was `NaN`**, from `∞/∞`. `y = d₁f/(d₁f + d₂)` and
  `1 − y` now come from one split that never overflows (`Beta.FisherSplit`).
- **The F density lost digits past shapes of a million.** It is now `Beta.Front(a, b, y, 1 − y) / f`,
  from Stirling's series. Measuring it against a 60-digit reference showed **scipy's own density is
  the one off there**, by `5e-7` at `(1e8, 1e8)` and `1e-5` at `(1e10, 1)`, so the corpus leaves
  those points out and a test holds them to the reference instead.
- **The F quantile lost `1 − y` when the root sits next to one**, `1e-7` at `(1e10, 1)`.
  `BetaQuantile.InvertBoth` returns both halves, refining by Newton on the small side only where the
  side formed by subtraction is below `1e-3`.
- Underneath: `Beta.Front` took `a log x` with `x` next to one, and formed `log B` from log-gammas
  of order `a log a` when only one shape is large (`Beta.LogBeta` now uses a Stirling increment);
  and `RegularizedIncomplete` returned one for `x == 1` even when `1 − x` still held digits.

## Proof

A new corpus, `tests/oracles/stats_distribution_functions.json`, generated by
`tools/generate_oracles.py` from `scipy.stats`, holds a grid for each of the twenty
(law, function) pairs. It covers the body, both far tails down to `1e-300` in probability, small
and large degrees of freedom, and the endpoints. It is replayed at `1e-9` **relative**, as the
existing distributions corpus is, since an absolute tolerance would accept zero for a tail of
`1e-24`. The existing `stats_distributions.json` corpus is left as it is and keeps passing, which
proves the five published members did not move.
