## The problem

The Review B after #1679, on `main` at `b9131663`, measured main against Lodestar.Preprocessing 0.2.0,
the pre-merge main `58b2ec7a`, scikit-learn 1.9.1, scipy 1.18.1 and numpy 2.5.3:

- `PowerTransformer` took the power's variance directly once it held more than 1e-8 of its mean square,
  which on a two-row column cancels to about 1e-13 relative from scipy's logarithms; the likelihood
  there is flat enough that the search stopped elsewhere, 32 of 400 Yeo-Johnson and 25 of 400 Box-Cox
  two-row transforms past 1e-5 of scikit-learn's (#1680);
- `OneHotEncoder.Transform` over strings ran 1.14 times 0.2.0's at 100,000 rows, a slowdown already in
  `58b2ec7a` (#1681);
- `MeanSquaredLogError.Score` ran 1.14 times its pre-merge time once it called the shared
  `NearZero.Log1P` rather than its own inlined copy (#1682);
- the encoders', `PowerTransformer`'s and the scalers' refusals printed the offending value in the
  current culture, `2,5` and `∞` under fr-FR (#1683);
- `Distributions.NormalQuantile` inverted the normal tail by Newton from an `erfc` good to 7e-15, so
  near one half its value strayed up to 5.5e-13 relative from `norm.ppf`, and `RobustScaler`'s unit
  variance divides by two of them (#1684).

## Decisions

- **scipy's `ndtri`, ported.** `Distributions.NormalQuantile` and `NormalIsf` take Cephes' rational
  approximations (Moshier, BSD via scipy's xsf, decision 0002) statement for statement; a Python
  port matched `scipy.special.ndtri` and `norm.ppf` on 240,099 points over both tails and the centre,
  and the Stats tests pin nine of them to the bit. The internal inverse other Stats members call
  (Shapiro-Wilk's Blom scores, the gamma quantile's seed) takes it too. Fligner-Killeen's normal scores
  keep Wichura's AS 241: on ndtri the `stats` harness timed the test at 9.59 ms against 8.34 at 100,000
  rows, its second logarithm, for statistics already within `1e-9` of scipy's. `RobustScaler` and `QuantileTransformer` reach it on
  the netstandard2.0 mirror now, which pins Lodestar.Stats by project, and on net10.0 at the next Stats
  publication, the suite building against the published floor.
- **Short power columns: a stated limit, not a code change.** Taking scipy's logarithmic variance below
  eight rows was tried and measured on 150 random columns per size: three to ten rows went from at most
  five columns past `1e-5` of scikit-learn's transform to at most one, and two rows from 72 Yeo-Johnson
  and 101 Box-Cox to 57 and 69, at 2.5 times the fit's time on a 7 × 50 matrix and 1.07–1.10 times on
  long Box-Cox columns through the JIT's layout. Two rows cannot follow scikit-learn at any cost short of
  a bit-identical objective: their optimum sits at `λ ≈ 0` and both optimisers stop on rounding noise,
  scikit-learn's exponents ranging from `1e-7` to `1e-4`. The time was kept, the maintainer's 1b of
  2026-10-10 stands, and `docs/equivalence.md` states the counts with the flat-likelihood limit.
- **One-hot columns are tabled at fit.** Bisecting put the slowdown at `595f3ece` (#1161), which worked out
  each value's grouped category and dropped column per value; a per-feature table of each category's final
  column, and of an unseen value's, built once, with the loop's bounds held in locals, brings `Transform`
  back. 100,000 rows × 3 features of 50 categories on an AMD Ryzen 7 8700G, the default tiered JIT, best
  of the last 200 runs after 60: over doubles the branch reads 12.8–13.5 ms against main's 12.3–12.9 and
  0.2.0's 14.2–14.5; over strings 9.9–10.7 against main's 9.8–10.4 and 0.2.0's 9.4–10.2, the time being
  mostly the 120 MB array each call allocates.
- **`NearZero.Log1P` inlines, branch-free past its zero test**: the `x > 1/ε` shortcut selects the factor
  rather than branching on the result, which took `MeanSquaredLogError` from 1.14 to 1.05 times its own
  copy's time, bit for bit the same values.
- **Refusals print their value in the invariant culture**, their words unchanged.
